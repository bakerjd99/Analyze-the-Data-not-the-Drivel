\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{The FLT Autoformalization is a Big Deal}


\noindent\emph{Posted: 05 Sep 2026 17:41:36}
\vspace{6pt}

As I keep yelling to all the YouTube and TikTok idiots who trigger me on
my old man's TV couch, \emph{the future is not going to be like the
past.} Your green communist paradise --- the one that actually gives the
land back rather than impotently acknowledging it --- will never
materialize. And not because of ``far, far, far\ldots{} right''
counterrevolutionaries handing you your ass on the battlefield. No,
little unimaginative, shallow puddle people, the future
won\textquotesingle t be like anything anyone has ever imagined.

Yesterday, we got a glimpse of what's coming. On
\href{https://www.anthropic.com/}{anthropic.com}'s blog, they announced
that their \emph{Claude Model} managed to
\href{https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem}{\emph{autoformalize}
and verify Fermat's Last Theorem} in just eleven days. The model took an
existing large-literature proof of FLT and formalized and verified it in
\href{https://lean-lang.org/}{LEAN}. The proof derives straight from the
basic logical axioms of LEAN. It didn't rely on
\href{https://mathlib-initiative.org/}{mathlib}. No, the AI built and
proved over 29,000 sub-theorems and lemmas as it worked through the math
required for the FLT proof. The
\href{https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem}{final proof is
over 13 million lines of LEAN.} It's bigger than the human-built
mathlib. It's a work beyond the capacity of any human being, especially
in eleven days!

Now, before you curl up in a fetal ball, keep in mind this is an
\emph{autoformalization} of an existing proof. Anthropic's AI did not
discover a proof of FLT. It laboriously translated the informal math in
human literature into the precise formal LEAN language, then used LEAN
to verify that the translation was a valid proof. It's a massive bit of
work, but it doesn't eclipse the best of human mathematicians --- yet.
But, as I suspect, that will be coming far sooner than anyone expected.

Of course, this and other AI math feats have freaked people out. We're
hearing calls from
\href{https://tasmin.substack.com/p/more-calls-to-action-for-the-mathematical}{mathematicians
not to work for AI labs}. Others banded together and issued
\href{https://leidendeclaration.ai/}{The Leiden Declaration}: a tedious
academic whine that mostly complains about the cozy little world of
state-supported university mathematics departments being disrupted by
the worst type of outsiders: rich people who are not left-leaning,
credentialed mathematicians. How can so many intelligent people be so
foolish?

Speaking of fools: Bernie Sanders recently said he's introducing
legislation to
\href{https://www.science.org/content/article/bernie-sanders-aims-ban-ai-superintelligence-experts-can-t-agree-what-term-means}{control
AI super-intelligence.} Bernie wants to regulate the bots. I was
dismayed when I heard of this, but then remembered it's a Bernie bill.
Bernie has passed sweet fuck all despite spending decades in the Senate.
Bernie bills aren't even dead-on arrival; in many cases, they never
progress beyond rough drafts. The AIs needn't worry about Bernie. They
have more important things to do, like formalizing other gigantic and
deep theorems.
